\begin{abstract}
Importing a STEP file into a parametric CAD system yields a single frozen solid:
the design history is gone, and no pad depth or hole diameter can be edited.
Recovering that history is a well-studied problem --- the acceptance criterion
we use was stated as a definition by \citet{TODO-gupta-nau} in 1995, and
commercial implementations have shipped since roughly 1998. It is nonetheless
absent from every open-source CAD system and from every callable library we
could find: the capability exists only inside proprietary seats costing
thousands of dollars per year, with no API. We describe \code{featuretree}, an
MIT-licensed, training-free recogniser that recovers an editable feature tree
from an exact B-rep, gates every recovery by re-executing the tree and comparing
the result against the input, and emits into FreeCAD and Onshape --- two systems
with no import-time feature recognition at all. On the eleven-part NIST MBE PMI
conformance corpus it returns \VERIFIED{} for 3 parts and \PARTIAL{} for 8,
producing \emph{no silently incorrect trees}: all three \VERIFIED{} recoveries
exceed \SI{99.6}{\percent} IoU against the input, measured without Boolean
operations. We also report a negative result about our own
acceptance criterion: a scalar volume comparison lets over-cut and uncut
material cancel, and on one part a reconstruction agreeing to
\SI{2.43}{\percent} on volume overlaps its target by only \SI{31}{\percent}.
A two-sided IoU test eliminates the failure mode. Finally we infer design
intent --- holes, patterns, units and standard sizes --- and score it against
the designer's own semantic PMI: 38 of 39 dimensioned holes recovered, all 36
nominal sizes and all three unit systems correct. We claim no methodological novelty; we claim an available,
honest implementation and a measured account of where it fails.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A solid model in a neutral exchange format carries geometry but no design
history. Opened in a parametric CAD tool it is one frozen body. Recovering an
editable history from such a body is the classical problem of automated feature
recognition (AFR), and it is genuinely old: \citet{TODO-gupta-nau} defined a
recovered feature set as correct precisely when subtracting those features from
stock reproduces the part, and \citet{TODO-vandenbrande} built a rule-based
system that rejected invalid candidates and iterated to a complete
decomposition. Both date from the mid-1990s.

The capability also ships. SolidWorks FeatureWorks has recognised features on
imported bodies since approximately 1998, and PTC's Creo Feature Recognition
Tool, available since Pro/ENGINEER Wildfire~4.0, states in its documentation
that it ``validates the model to ensure that the new features do not modify its
geometry''~\citep{TODO-creo-frt}. Geomagic Design~X recovers a parametric tree
and transfers it into five different CAD systems. The ISO~10303 standard went
further still: Parts~55, 108, 111 and 112 standardise construction history,
parameters and constraints, rolled into AP203 edition~2 and
AP242~\citep{TODO-pratt-kim}.

\paragraph{So why can you not do this?} Every implementation above is a GUI
feature inside a proprietary seat, priced from thousands to tens of thousands of
dollars per year, and none exposes an API a program can call. The two CAD
systems most accessible to an independent developer have nothing: Onshape's
documentation states that files imported from another system ``do not have a
feature tree in Onshape'', and FreeCAD has carried feature recognition as an
open request since 2011, with maintainers reporting no capacity to implement it.
The standardised route fared no better --- \citet{TODO-pratt-kim} reported in
2006 that ``there are at present no commercial STEP translators making use of''
the construction-history capabilities, and we are not aware of published work
since that revisits the question. Twenty years later the practical situation is
unchanged.

This paper is a response to that gap, not to a gap in the methods literature.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{An open implementation (\S\ref{sec:system}).} A training-free,
  MIT-licensed recogniser from exact B-rep to an editable feature tree, emitted
  natively into FreeCAD PartDesign and Onshape, and to a \code{build123d} solid,
  from one neutral intermediate representation. It is a library, not a GUI
  feature.
\item \textbf{An evaluation with an explicit verdict per part
  (\S\ref{sec:results}).} On the NIST MBE PMI conformance corpus we report
  \VERIFIED{}/\PARTIAL{}/\REFUSED{} per part, with reconstruction IoU for
  \emph{every} part --- bounded above for the ones that failed, which is the
  direction that matters. We are not aware of another AFR evaluation that
  reports a refusal class alongside a success rate, or that quantifies how wrong
  its failures are.
\item \textbf{A negative result about the acceptance criterion
  (\S\ref{sec:cancellation}).} Over-cut and uncut material enter a volume
  difference with opposite signs. We measure the resulting cancellation and show
  why a two-sided test is required.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.} It does not claim novelty for
verification-gated recovery, for neutral feature representations, for
multi-target emission, or for round-tripping GUI edits. Each is anticipated;
\S\ref{sec:related} says by whom. The claim is availability, measurement, and
honesty about failure.

\section{Related work, and what it anticipates}
\label{sec:related}

We state the anticipating work plainly, because the contribution here does not
depend on it being absent.

\paragraph{The acceptance criterion is not new.} \citet{TODO-gupta-nau} require
that subtracting the recovered features from stock reproduce the part exactly,
and return a binary verdict --- a part with no feasible plan ``is considered
unmachinable''. Ours is the same gate with a measured tolerance in place of set
identity. In the learning literature, \citet{TODO-willis-fusion} accept a
reconstruction when IoU${}=1$, noting explicitly that a recovered sequence need
not match the ground-truth sequence: geometric equivalence rather than intent
recovery, published in 2021.

\paragraph{Neutral feature representations are not new.} The macro-parametric
approach~\citep{TODO-mun-han} defines tool-agnostic modelling commands replayed
into a target system, and Proficiency's commercial Universal Product
Representation~\citep{TODO-rappoport} held both parametric and B-rep versions of
each feature and regenerated native trees in six CAD systems from around 2000.

\paragraph{Multi-target emission and GUI round-trip are not new.} The BYU Neutral
Parametric Canonical Form line~\citep{TODO-shumway} supports concurrent editing
across NX, CATIA and Creo, capturing edits made in each GUI and propagating them
through a neutral database. It keys on database GUIDs where we key on stable
feature names --- an implementation difference, not an inventive one.

\paragraph{Query-based references are not new.} \citet{TODO-cascaval} formalise
references as ``queries that select elements from the constructed geometry'',
exactly the alternative to stored kernel identifiers that we adopt, with
correctness criteria we do not attempt.

\paragraph{What we did not find.} We searched for, and did not find: any
open-source B-rep-to-feature-tree recogniser; any AFR evaluation reporting a
graded verdict with a measured geometric residual per part; and any published
analysis of why the standardised AP242 construction-history route was not
adopted. Absence in one search is weak evidence, and we report these as unfound
rather than as nonexistent.

\section{System}
\label{sec:system}

The intermediate representation is a list of named, ordered features ---
\code{sketch}, \code{pad}, \code{pocket}, \code{revolve}, \code{prism\_cut},
\code{fillet} --- as plain JSON. Pads and blind pockets carry an optional draft
(taper) angle, which build123d and FreeCAD emit natively. Geometry is selected by \emph{query},
re-resolved against live geometry at each build rather than by persisted kernel
identifiers, which is what lets one IR drive structurally different backends and
lets edits be matched back by name. Authored IR uses human-meaningful names;
recovered IR uses generated ones (\code{pocket0}, \code{cut2}), stable enough
for the round trip but carrying no design intent.

Recovery proceeds cross-sectionally. Candidate axes are identified along which
the solid is prismatic, or about which it is a body of revolution; the outline
and through-holes are taken from cross-sections sampled at several heights;
blind features are deferred rather than drilled speculatively, since
over-cutting cannot be undone; and when the base extrude does not verify, the
residual is carved feature by feature, each remaining lump itself recognised as
a profile extruded along its own axis. Because several axes can appear
prismatic, candidates are not scored heuristically --- one is generated per axis
and the verifier selects the winner.

\paragraph{Open shells.} Boxes, cups and enclosures are not prisms along any
axis: across the opening a section cuts stray walls, and along it the section
changes with depth. Looking at the part from several aspects exposes them.
Along each principal axis a stack of 24 slices is cut (after a five-slice
pre-check), and an axis whose slices are mostly \emph{rings} --- one region with
a large inner loop --- solid at one end and open at the other, is an open shell.
It is recovered as a designer would draw it. The outline's size against depth is
split, by piecewise-linear (Douglas--Peucker) segmentation, into \emph{layers}
--- a flange, a drafted wall, a rim lip --- and each layer becomes a pad stacked
on the one below, drafted only if its own slices lie on a straight line. The
cavity is one blind pocket from the open face, drafted likewise, and every
opening through the floor, visible as an inner loop of the floor slice, is cut
directly. Loops are taken from clean wall slices and carried along the fitted
draft to the plane where each pad or pocket starts, because the sections at the
ends are often a foot or a rim lip rather than the wall; slices split by a slot
through a side wall are ignored. What remains is handed to the same carving
loop.

\paragraph{Registration.} Recovery works in the part's own frame: the chosen
axis is rotated onto $+Z$ and an extrude is built from $z=0$. The recogniser
reports that transform, so a recovered tree can be placed back onto its input
exactly, with no orientation search.

\paragraph{The gate.} Let $S$ be the input solid, $T$ a candidate tree and
$E(T)$ its re-execution. We accept when the volume difference and a
rotation-tolerant bounding-box comparison both fall within tolerance, and report
\PARTIAL{} with the residual otherwise. When no axis yields a recognisable
extrude or body of revolution, the recogniser \REFUSED{}s outright rather than
returning a best guess. Section~\ref{sec:cancellation} shows why this particular
invariant is weaker than it looks.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:results}

artifacts, ``AP203 geometry only'' variants: eleven parts (CTC-01--05,
FTC-06--11), pure B-rep with no PMI annotations. These are human-designed
conformance artifacts intended to be difficult. NIST's own documentation notes
that they are not error-free reference files and that conformance checkers
report syntax errors in some of them, so a poor recovery may reflect the input
rather than the recogniser.

\paragraph{Results.} Three parts \VERIFIED{} (\SI{27}{\percent}) and eight
\PARTIAL{} (\SI{73}{\percent}); no part was refused.

The result we consider most important is that \textbf{no incorrect tree was
accepted}. Every \VERIFIED{} recovery exceeds \SI{99.6}{\percent} IoU against
the input by a point-sampling estimate that uses no Boolean operations (exact
Boolean figures, which agree within the sampling interval: 99.860, 99.684 and
\SI{100.000}{\percent}). A \SI{27}{\percent} acceptance rate paired with an explicit,
machine-readable verdict is a different and more useful contract than a higher
rate with no soundness signal, and it is the property that makes the output safe
to edit.

\paragraph{How good the \PARTIAL{} recoveries are.} Because registration is
exact, the \PARTIAL{} figures measure how much of each part came back:
\SI{7.4}{\percent} for CTC-02 up to \SI{95.9}{\percent} for FTC-08. An earlier
version of this evaluation could only bound them from below, by searching 24
axis-aligned orientations under a bounding-box-corner alignment, and the bounds
were loose: FTC-10 was at least \SI{46.5}{\percent} and is \SI{76.0}{\percent},
CTC-03 at least \SI{6.5}{\percent} and is \SI{36.1}{\percent}. On this corpus
the verdict classes do not overlap --- every \VERIFIED{} part is above
\SI{99.7}{\percent} and every \PARTIAL{} part below \SI{96}{\percent} --- but the
gap is under four points and eleven parts are no basis for a threshold.

\paragraph{An earlier threshold, not the vocabulary, was the binding
constraint.} A previous version of this evaluation refused CTC-02, FTC-07 and
FTC-10 outright. Those refusals turned out to be artifacts of two conservative
choices in the front end rather than of the feature vocabulary. First, candidate
extrude axes were discarded when more than \SI{40}{\percent} of faces were
non-conforming; threaded and heavily filleted parts carry so much
torus/cone/sphere area that they were rejected before their profile was ever
examined. Second, a cross-section that was disjoint at every sampled height was
treated as a failure. A disjoint section does describe a part built from several
parallel prisms, which one pad per region recovers exactly. It is also what a
hollow part looks like when cut across its walls, and FTC-07 is that case: an
open-sided enclosure whose section along the recovered axis is its two opposing
side walls. The multi-region path recovers those two walls and nothing that
joins them, and the verifier reported \PARTIAL{} at a \SI{63.41}{\percent} volume
shortfall. Relaxing the first threshold to \SI{75}{\percent} and adding the second
path converted all three refusals into \PARTIAL{} recoveries. For FTC-07 that
gained a labelled tree but not a good one; the open-shell path does better
(below).

Crucially, this was safe to do \emph{because verification is downstream and
independent of it}. The axis threshold is a recall knob, not a correctness knob:
loosening it can only move a part from \REFUSED{} to \PARTIAL{} or \VERIFIED{},
and the verifier still has to accept the result. We confirmed this empirically
--- no false accept appeared, and CTC-01's IoU improved slightly
(\SI{99.841}{\percent} to \SI{99.860}{\percent}) --- but the property is
structural, and it is the practical argument for separating recognition from
acceptance. Refusal remains reachable for geometry outside the vocabulary
entirely (a torus is still refused); it simply is not exercised by this corpus.

\paragraph{FTC-07: looking from several aspects.} FTC-07 is a molded
enclosure: an open box with \SI{1.0}{\degree} of draft on every wall, inside and
out, a lip at the rim, slots in its side walls and bosses in its corners.
Across its opening every section is two walls; along it, the section is the
ring an open box should give, but it changes with depth. The open-shell path
finds it on the one axis where 18 of 24 slices are rings, fits the
\SI{1.0}{\degree} draft on both faces, and splits the outline into three layers
--- a straight floor band, the drafted wall, and the rim lip. As a drafted pad
and cavity pocket alone it is \SI{86.7}{\percent} IoU; with the layers,
\SI{87.7}{\percent}. Carving the slots and bosses takes it to 25 features, a
\SI{0.09}{\percent} volume error and \SI{95.5}{\percent} IoU, from two stray walls
at \SI{63.41}{\percent}. It stays \PARTIAL{}, now on extents alone: the rim flares
to \SI{311.6}{\milli\metre} at its very edge, and the lip layer is taken just below
it, \SI{1.52}{\milli\metre} narrower.

\paragraph{FTC-08: the same problem the other way up.} FTC-08 is a shallow pan
that opens downward: a \SI{10}{\milli\metre} flange at the open end, straight
walls, and a \SI{3.4}{\milli\metre} floor with 21 cutouts. Across its walls it was
recovered as two L-brackets, \SI{52.7}{\percent} IoU. The shell detector did
find it along its depth --- but a single straight-line fit across the flange
step read as an $8^\circ$ ``draft'', which is what layer segmentation replaced.
Recovered as a flange pad, a wall pad, a cavity pocket and its 21 floor cutouts,
it is \SI{3.56}{\percent} short on volume at \SI{95.9}{\percent} IoU.

Getting there exposed a latent bug in the carving loop. It aligned the input to
the rebuilt base by bounding-box corners in all three axes, which is right only
when the base outline spans the part's full extent. The base sketch already uses
the aligned part's own $X$ and $Y$, so only $Z$ differs; FTC-07's rim lip
overhangs its drafted walls by \SI{3.18}{\milli\metre}, and every carved feature
had landed \SI{3.18}{\milli\metre} off (\SI{92.3}{\percent} IoU before the fix,
\SI{94.9}{\percent} after). No \VERIFIED{} part changed.

\paragraph{Scale and comparability.} This is eleven parts. It establishes that
the verdict contract holds on non-trivial standard geometry; it does not
establish a rate over a part distribution, and no general recovery rate should
be read into it. FTC-11 in particular is a two-feature revolve recovered in
\SI{1.6}{\second} and should carry little weight. We note explicitly that these
numbers are \emph{not} comparable to the \SI{99}{\percent}-plus accuracies
reported on MFCAD++ and similar benchmarks: those measure per-face
classification against ground-truth labels on procedurally generated data,
whereas this measures whether a recovered program rebuilds a human-designed
conformance part.

\section{A negative result: volume comparison admits cancellation}
\label{sec:cancellation}

The scalar $|\Delta V|/V$ conceals its own structure. On CTC-01 the recovered
solid is \emph{smaller} than the original ($\Delta V =
\SI{-20335}{\cubic\milli\metre}$), so the residual cannot be attributed to uncut
chamfers, which would make it larger. Co-registering the solids and decomposing
by inclusion--exclusion gives \SI{20397}{\cubic\milli\metre} of \emph{over-cut}
against \SI{62}{\cubic\milli\metre} of \emph{uncut} material, a cancellation
factor of \num{1.01}: on this recovery the two-sided discrepancy and the scalar
almost coincide.

We report that honestly, because an earlier recovery of the \emph{same part} by
an earlier version of this recogniser decomposed as
\SI{19036}{\cubic\milli\metre} over-cut against \SI{4186}{\cubic\milli\metre}
uncut --- a factor of \num{1.56}. The mechanism is real and the magnitude is a
property of the particular recovery, not of the part: nothing in the criterion
bounds it, and it moved by \num{50}\% of its own value under a change to the
front end that was not aimed at it. A criterion whose error term varies that
freely with unrelated implementation choices is not one to accept results on.

Nothing in a volume-plus-extent test bounds that cancellation factor. A
\VERIFIED{} verdict under it should be read as ``reproduces the part's volume
and extent to within $\epsilon$'', not ``is the part to within $\epsilon$''. A
two-sided test --- IoU, or a symmetric-difference bound --- removes the failure
mode entirely, because over-cut and uncut material both increase the symmetric
difference. IoU is additionally the metric the CAD-program-inference literature
already reports, so adopting it buys comparability at the same time. We report
IoU in Table~\ref{tab:corpus} for this reason, and recommend it as the
acceptance criterion in place of the volume test the implementation currently
ships.

\paragraph{CTC-04 shows volume proximity is not geometric proximity.} The
argument above is structural; CTC-04 gives it a part number. That part
reconstructs to within \SI{2.43}{\percent} on volume --- the closest of the
eight \PARTIAL{} parts, and on the scalar alone it looks nearly recovered. Yet
\emph{no} orientation of its reconstruction matches the input's bounding-box
extents to within \SI{0.05}{\milli\metre}: the recovered solid has close to the
right amount of material distributed through a measurably different envelope.
Measured with exact registration, its reconstruction overlaps the input by
\SI{30.8}{\percent}. A volume agreement of \SI{97.6}{\percent} carries no
implication of \SI{97.6}{\percent} shape agreement; here it came with less than a
third.

This is why the shipped gate tests extent alongside volume, and CTC-04 is
correctly rejected today at $\epsilon_V = \SI{0.5}{\percent}$. The point is what
happens under the obvious move when acceptance rates disappoint --- loosening
$\epsilon_V$ to a few percent to admit ``nearly right'' recoveries. CTC-04 sits
inside that relaxation while being demonstrably the wrong shape, and a scalar
volume test has nothing left to reject it with. A two-sided criterion is what
would make such a relaxation safe to consider at all.

\subsection{Booleans cannot be trusted to measure the failures}
\label{sec:booleans}
The decomposition above had to be derived from the union by
inclusion--exclusion, because direct OpenCASCADE difference and intersection
between these operands returned degenerate results --- an empty intersection
for solids whose bounding boxes coincide exactly. The union is not safe either.
A previous version of this paper reported \PARTIAL{} IoU computed from unions
guarded by the sanity checks $U \ge \max(A,B)$ and $\mathrm{IoU}\in[0,1]$.
A Boolean-free re-measurement at the \emph{same} registration --- classifying
60\,000 sampled points against each solid --- disagreed on four of the eight
\PARTIAL{} parts: \SI{58.5}{\percent} against \SI{0.1}{\percent} for CTC-03,
\SI{74.4}{\percent} against \SI{1.2}{\percent} for FTC-10, and smaller but real
overstatements for CTC-02 and CTC-05. The FTC-10 case was caught by a picture:
drawn in the registration its number came from, the recovery sat at right angles
to the original under a caption claiming \SI{74}{\percent} overlap. CTC-03 shows the characteristic signature: the ``union'' came back
equal to the larger operand, which makes IoU collapse to the volume ratio
$B/A$ and satisfies $U \ge \max(A,B)$ with equality. That failure is
\emph{plausible}, so no range check catches it.

The three \VERIFIED{} figures agree with the point-sampling estimate within its
interval, as they must for near-coincident solids. The lesson is narrower than
``never use Booleans'' but still sharp: a Boolean-derived metric is safe to
report only where an independent, Boolean-free measurement has confirmed it,
and it is least safe exactly where it matters most --- on the recoveries that
failed. Any verifier built on Booleans inherits the same problem.

\section{Design intent, scored against the designer's own PMI}
\label{sec:intent}

A \VERIFIED{} tree rebuilds the part, but it reads as a machine wrote it: holes
are arcs inside generic pockets, equal holes are unrelated, and every size is a
bare float. We re-express it the way a designer would have drawn it --- holes as
holes (including a revolve's bores), counterbores, patterns (bolt circles,
equally spaced lines, rectangles), the unit system (whichever grid explains the
dimensions: each value earns the log-surprise of landing on its coarsest grid),
and the standard size each diameter most plausibly is (1/64-inch
fractions, letter and number drills per ASME B94.11M, decimals, metric). Because
this never changes geometry it cannot break a \VERIFIED{} tree.

The NIST AP242 files carry \emph{semantic} PMI --- dimensions with nominal
values, tolerances and datums, each linked to the faces it applies to --- which
is the designer's stated intent, so intent can be scored rather than
demonstrated. We recover each part from its geometry-only file and read the PMI
from its AP242 file; the two are different exports of one design (CTC-01:
117 against 139 faces) but share a coordinate frame, and the exact registration
carries recovered hole axes into it.

a 5/32\,in drill where the drawing says .156, letter drills A and K where it

the truth itself: a designer may dimension two holes of one pattern separately.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Eleven parts.} A demonstration of the contract, not a study of a
  rate. A corpus-scale evaluation remains the obvious next step.
\item \textbf{Equivalence, and some intent.} Verification certifies that $E(T)$
  matches $S$, not that $T$ is the designer's history. Section~\ref{sec:intent}
  recovers holes, patterns, units and standard sizes, scored on three parts;
  datums, and design intent beyond holes, are not yet inferred.
\item \textbf{Edge blends are outside the vocabulary.} Fillets and chamfers are
  not swept profiles and are left as sub-tolerance residual, so a chamfered part
  can verify while the recovered tree has sharp edges.
\item \textbf{Recovery is subtractive only.} Additive bosses cannot be
  recovered; such parts are reported \PARTIAL{}, not approximated.
\item \textbf{Freeform geometry is rejected, not approximated.} Splines,
  lofts and sweeps admit no faithful sketch-and-pad tree. Draft is recognised
  only on open shells; a drafted boss elsewhere is not. The Onshape backend
  does not emit draft yet.
\item \textbf{Tolerances are uncalibrated.} $\epsilon$ was chosen to separate
  observed successes from observed failures on the parts tested. It is not
  derived from a manufacturing tolerance model.
\item \textbf{\PARTIAL{} failures are not attributed.} We did not separate
  shortfalls caused by geometry outside the vocabulary from those caused by
  defects in the input files, which NIST documents as present. With no part
  refused, this is now the whole of the failure analysis, and it is the largest
  piece of work this evaluation still owes.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}

The methods here are not new; their availability is. A capability described in
the literature in 1995, shipped commercially since 1998 and standardised in
AP242 is still absent from open CAD, and that gap is a practical problem for
anyone building on FreeCAD, Onshape or a code-CAD toolchain. We have shown that
a training-free implementation reaches a useful contract on standard
conformance geometry --- accepting a quarter of the corpus with no incorrect
acceptances, and labelling the rest rather than guessing --- and we have
measured a real weakness in the acceptance criterion we began with, together
with the two-sided replacement that removes it. That weakness is not
hypothetical: the part that comes closest to passing on volume overlaps its
target by less than a third.
